Headache and Migraine

Migraine is a neurological condition. It is set off by certain triggers and often runs in families.

Common triggers

Work stress, exams, pain elsewhere in the body, hunger, bright or poor-quality light, neck and shoulder pain, irregular sleep, and certain foods and smells.

Symptoms

  • Headache on one side, which can spread or switch sides
  • Severe headache that worsens with light or sound
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Seeing black spots or flashes of light
Seek urgent care for a sudden, severe "worst ever" headache, or headache with fever and neck stiffness, weakness, confusion or loss of vision.

Treatment

  • Fewer than 2 attacks a month: medicines taken at the time of the attack
  • More than 2 attacks a month: daily medicines to prevent attacks
  • 15 or more headache days a month despite medicines: Botox injections may be considered
  • Lifestyle changes to avoid triggers

Some headaches come from the nose and sinuses, such as sinusitis or a deviated septum, and an ENT examination helps tell these apart.
