Ear Problems and Endoscopic Ear Surgery

Our lead surgeon has performed endoscopic ear surgery since 2010, and endoscopic interlay tympanoplasty since 2023. Experience

Hearing less? Take the free Hearing Check, with a simple hum test, to see how urgently to be seen. Sudden nerve hearing loss has the best result when treatment starts within 3 days.

What ear discharge can tell us

  • Watery: usually from the skin of the ear canal, and not serious
  • Pus: infection of the outer or middle ear
  • Sticky (mucoid): usually means a hole in the eardrum

Hearing loss

  • Gradual hearing loss is common with age, as the hearing nerve weakens.
  • Blockage of the ear canal, such as wax, can reduce hearing.
  • A hole in the eardrum causes hearing loss.
Sudden hearing loss is an emergency. Sudden sensorineural hearing loss can often be treated if treatment starts early, but may become permanent if delayed. See an ENT doctor the same day.

Vertigo

Vertigo can come from the inner ear. Read more about vertigo.

When is ear surgery needed?

Most ear problems are treated with medicines first. Surgery is needed when a hole in the eardrum doesn't heal, the ear bones don't move properly, or there is disease eroding bone.

How is ear surgery done?

Using either a microscope or an endoscope, with very fine instruments. Most ear operations are done as day-care surgery.

Microscopic or endoscopic ear surgery?

Both give similar success rates. Microscopic surgery usually needs a cut behind or in front of the ear for access. Endoscopic surgery is done through the ear canal, and any small cut needed for the graft is placed in a hidden area.

What can be done endoscopically?

  • Closing a hole in the eardrum (tympanoplasty), including endoscopic interlay tympanoplasty
  • Draining fluid from the middle ear (myringotomy)
  • Repairing the ear bones (ossiculoplasty)
  • Removing limited bone-eroding disease (cholesteatoma)

Other ear operations we perform

  • Hole in the eardrum: usually closed by endoscopic tympanoplasty (interlay or underlay), using a graft from the covering of a muscle or from cartilage. Small holes can be closed with a fat graft or PRF (see below). Holes with a weak or pulled-in eardrum may need a cartilage graft, which is stronger.
  • Otosclerosis (hearing loss from a stiff ear bone, often in young adults): stapedotomy, a fine operation to restore sound transmission.
  • Fluid behind the eardrum (glue ear): a tiny ventilation tube (grommet) when fluid doesn't clear with treatment.
  • Bone-eroding disease (cholesteatoma): mastoidectomy, planned to remove all disease while keeping as much of the ear's structure as possible.
  • Damaged ear bones: ossicular reconstruction, sometimes in two stages.

Endoscopic interlay tympanoplasty

The eardrum has three thin layers. In the usual method (underlay), the graft that closes the hole is placed underneath the whole eardrum. In the interlay method, the graft is slipped between the outer skin layer and the middle layer of the eardrum, where it sits very securely.

  • Done through the ear canal with an endoscope, so no cut is needed behind the ear for access.
  • The graft is usually your own tissue (temporalis fascia), taken through a small cut hidden in the hairline. In selected small holes, PRF (a healing membrane made from your own blood) may be used.
  • The method is chosen during surgery: interlay is used when the ear bones are working well. If they aren't, we use the underlay method and repair the ear bones at the same time.
  • Usually done as day-care surgery.

Published studies of endoscopic interlay tympanoplasty report high rates of successful closure and good hearing results. It is technically demanding, so it is offered at relatively few centres.

A blocked or poorly working Eustachian tube can make holes in the eardrum come back. In selected patients, balloon Eustachian tube dilation may be advised as well.

Fat and PRF myringoplasty

Fat myringoplasty is a simple way to close a small hole in the eardrum. A tiny piece of fat is taken from the earlobe and placed into the hole through the ear canal, where it acts as a plug that the eardrum heals over. It is done under local anaesthesia as day care, with no cut behind the ear. It suits small, dry holes. Larger holes need a tympanoplasty.

PRF myringoplasty uses platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) made from a small sample of your own blood. PRF is rich in the body's natural healing factors. Placed on a small hole, alone or together with fat, it helps the edges of the eardrum grow and close. It is also done under local anaesthesia. Because it comes from your own blood, there is no risk of a reaction.

How long is the rest after ear surgery?

Usually 4–6 weeks of avoiding strain and keeping water out of the ear. After myringotomy alone, no rest is needed.
