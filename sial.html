Sialendoscopy for Salivary Gland Problems

Sialendoscopy uses a very thin endoscope passed through the natural opening of the salivary duct inside the mouth, to diagnose and treat salivary gland problems without removing the gland.

Symptoms of salivary gland problems

Pain and swelling under the jaw or in front of the ear, often while eating; a dry mouth; or repeated infections of the gland.

Conditions treated

Salivary stones (sialolithiasis)

Hard deposits form in the gland or duct and block the flow of saliva, causing pain and swelling at mealtimes.

  • Tests: ultrasound scan, and sometimes a CT scan
  • Treatment: small stones can be removed through the endoscope with a tiny basket; larger stones may need a small cut inside the mouth or stone fragmentation

Duct narrowing (stricture)

Narrowing of the duct slows saliva flow and leads to repeated swelling and infection.

  • Tests: ultrasound, and sialendoscopy to see the narrowing directly
  • Treatment: widening the duct through the endoscope, with steroid rinses

Chronic inflammation and recurrent infection

Repeated swelling from inflammation, infections or autoimmune disease.

  • Tests: ultrasound and blood tests where an autoimmune cause is suspected
  • Treatment: washing out the duct and steroid irrigation through the endoscope, with medical treatment of the cause

Benefits

Sialendoscopy aims to preserve the gland, avoids a cut on the face or neck in most cases, and usually allows going home the same day.
